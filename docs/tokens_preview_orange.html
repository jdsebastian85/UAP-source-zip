The palette

Warm charcoal ground, one orange accent. Same token architecture the tool already uses, so applying it stays a value swap rather than a rewrite.

The decision this forces

Amber was reserved to mean one thing: a page that could not be read. An orange accent takes that signal away — at 11px a tick cannot be “orange, but not that orange.” Three ways out. The comb below is the same 34 pages in all three: 24 readable, 4 low-legibility, 6 unreadable.

Brand and comb both orange. Reads well as a page and is the most obviously “on-brand”.

✕ The unreadable tick stops being an alarm. It is the same family as the nav, the buttons and the worked ticks, so the eye stops picking it out — which is the one job it has.

B · Orange is the alarm

comb goes cool, orange reserved
text layer low legibilityunreadable

The comb is steel; orange appears in the evidence layer only to mean a page nobody can read. The chrome keeps orange too, but the evidence layer holds exactly one saturated colour.

✓ Keeps your locked rule intact with the hue swapped: orange now means unreadable. The gap is the loudest thing on the page, which is the argument the whole tool makes.

C · Absence as absence

no hue reserved at all
text layer low legibilityunreadable

Unreadable is drawn as a hatched void rather than a colour. Nothing is reserved, so orange is free everywhere else.

◐ Most literal — absence rendered as absence, no colour key to learn. But hatching at 11px is weak on a phone in daylight, and Wave 2 stacks a second comb row right above this one.

Chrome and evidence, side by side

Glass and depth on the chrome only. The evidence layer stays flat, opaque and high contrast — that discipline does not change with the palette.
